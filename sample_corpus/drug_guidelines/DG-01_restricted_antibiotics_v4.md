## 1 Purpose

This policy classifies antimicrobials used in DHN hospitals as unrestricted, restricted or reserve, and describes the approval each class needs. It supports antimicrobial stewardship (AMS) by making sure broad-spectrum and last-line agents are used only when they are needed.

## 2 Definitions

- **Unrestricted:** may be prescribed by any prescriber in line with DHN treatment guidelines.
- **Restricted:** needs approval from the AMS pharmacist or an Infectious Diseases (ID) consultant within 24 hours of the first dose. The first dose must not be delayed while approval is sought.
- **Reserve:** needs approval from an ID consultant before the first dose, except in septic shock (see section 4.2).

## 3 Classification

### 3.1 Unrestricted antimicrobials

The following agents are unrestricted when used in line with DHN treatment guidelines.

| Agent | Route | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin-clavulanate | IV or oral | First line for community-acquired intra-abdominal infection |
| Ceftriaxone | IV | First line for community-acquired pneumonia requiring admission |
| Piperacillin-tazobactam | IV | Hospital-acquired infection and neutropenic sepsis |
| Azithromycin | IV or oral | Atypical pneumonia cover |
| Metronidazole | IV or oral | Anaerobic cover |
| Doxycycline | Oral | Atypical cover and skin infections |

### 3.2 Restricted antimicrobials

The following agents are restricted and need AMS pharmacist or ID consultant approval within 24 hours of the first dose.

| Agent | Route | Typical indication |
|---|---|---|
| Meropenem | IV | Suspected ESBL-producing organisms or failure of first-line therapy |
| Vancomycin | IV | MRSA infection or severe beta-lactam allergy |
| Linezolid | IV or oral | VRE or MRSA when vancomycin is unsuitable |
| Teicoplanin | IV | MRSA infection when vancomycin is unsuitable |
| Cefepime | IV | Pseudomonas cover when carbapenems are to be spared |

### 3.3 Reserve antimicrobials

Colistin, polymyxin B, tigecycline, ceftazidime-avibactam and cefiderocol are reserve agents. They need approval from an ID consultant before the first dose, and each course is reviewed by the AMS team every 48 hours.

## 4 Approval process

### 4.1 Restricted agents

Complete the AMS approval form in the electronic prescribing system when the first dose is prescribed. The AMS pharmacist (extension 2240, 08:00–20:00) reviews the request; outside these hours the on-call microbiologist reviews it. Approval must be recorded within 24 hours of the first dose, otherwise pharmacy contacts the prescribing team.

### 4.2 Reserve agents

Discuss every reserve agent with the on-call ID consultant before prescribing. In septic shock the first dose may be given before approval, but the ID consultant must approve the course within 12 hours of the first dose.

## 5 Review and de-escalation

Review every intravenous antimicrobial at 48 to 72 hours (the "antimicrobial time-out"). Record the review decision — stop, switch to oral, narrow the spectrum, or continue with a stated duration — in the medical notes.
