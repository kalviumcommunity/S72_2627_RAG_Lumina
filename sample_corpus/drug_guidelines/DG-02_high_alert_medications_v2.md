## 1 Purpose

High-alert medications carry a heightened risk of causing significant harm when used in error. This guideline lists the high-alert medications used in DHN hospitals and the safeguards that apply to them.

## 2 High-alert medication list

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Anticoagulants | Unfractionated heparin, enoxaparin, warfarin |
| Insulins | All insulins, including intravenous insulin infusions |
| Concentrated electrolytes | Potassium chloride concentrate, sodium chloride 3%, magnesium sulfate 50% |
| Opioids | Intravenous and epidural opioids, fentanyl patches |
| Neuromuscular blocking agents | Atracurium, rocuronium, suxamethonium |
| Chemotherapy | All cytotoxic agents |

## 3 General safeguards

### 3.1 Independent double check

Two registered nurses, or a registered nurse and a doctor, independently check every high-alert infusion at the start, at every rate change and at every bag or syringe change. Each checker confirms the drug, the concentration, the pump programme and the patient's identity separately before signing the chart.

### 3.2 Storage

Concentrated potassium chloride ampoules are stored only in pharmacy and in locked controlled cabinets in intensive care units. They must never be stored on general wards. Neuromuscular blocking agents are stored separately from other medicines in labelled, segregated boxes.

## 4 Drug-specific rules

### 4.1 Potassium chloride

- The maximum infusion rate through a peripheral line is 10 mmol/h, at a concentration no higher than 40 mmol/L.
- Rates up to 20 mmol/h may be given only through a central line in an intensive care or high dependency unit with continuous cardiac monitoring.
- Never give potassium chloride as an intravenous push or bolus.

### 4.2 Heparin infusions

- Use only pharmacy-prepared or pre-mixed heparin syringes (25,000 units in 50 mL).
- For heparin infusions, check the aPTT 4 hours after any rate change.
- Stop the infusion and seek advice from the ICU registrar if the aPTT is above 150 seconds.

### 4.3 Insulin

- Always write the word "units" in full; never abbreviate it to "U" or "IU".
- Intravenous insulin infusions use 50 units of soluble insulin in 50 mL sodium chloride 0.9% (1 unit/mL).
- Check the capillary blood glucose every hour while an insulin infusion is running.

## 5 Reporting

Report any error or near miss involving a high-alert medication on the DHN incident reporting system within 24 hours, and inform the nurse in charge and the prescriber immediately.
