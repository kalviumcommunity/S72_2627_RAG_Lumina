## 1 Purpose

This protocol sets out how DHN staff recognise sepsis in adults and deliver the time-critical elements of the one-hour sepsis bundle, so that every patient with suspected sepsis receives the same assessment and treatment regardless of branch, ward or time of day.

## 2 Scope

The protocol applies to adults (16 years and older) in DHN emergency departments and adult inpatient wards. It does not apply to pregnant or recently pregnant women, who follow the maternity sepsis pathway, or to neonates and children, who follow the paediatric sepsis pathway.

## 3 Recognition

### 3.1 Screening triggers

Screen for sepsis any adult with a suspected infection who has any one of the following: a NEWS2 score of 5 or more; a systolic blood pressure of 100 mmHg or less; a respiratory rate of 22 breaths/min or more; new confusion or altered mental state; or a lactate of 2 mmol/L or more.

### 3.2 Septic shock

Treat as septic shock any patient with sepsis who needs vasopressors to keep the mean arterial pressure (MAP) at 65 mmHg or above and whose lactate remains above 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation. Septic shock requires immediate senior review and referral to critical care.

## 4 Immediate actions

Within 15 minutes of a positive screen, the nurse informs the doctor responsible, records the time of recognition ("time zero") on the sepsis screening form, starts high-flow oxygen if the oxygen saturation is below 94%, and obtains intravenous access.

## 5 The one-hour bundle

### 5.1 Lactate

Measure a venous or arterial lactate at time zero. Remeasure the lactate within 2 hours if the initial result is 2 mmol/L or more, and use the trend to guide further fluid resuscitation.

### 5.2 Blood cultures

Take at least two sets of blood cultures before the first dose of antibiotics, provided this does not delay antibiotics by more than 45 minutes. Send other cultures (urine, sputum, wound swabs) as clinically indicated.

### 5.3 Antibiotics

Give broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics within 1 hour of sepsis recognition to every patient with suspected sepsis. Choose the agent according to DG-01 (Restricted Antimicrobials Policy) and the local antibiogram, and record the time of the first dose.

### 5.4 Fluids

For hypotension or a lactate of 4 mmol/L or more, give 30 mL/kg of intravenous crystalloid within the first 3 hours. Give the fluid as 500 mL boluses over 15 minutes and reassess the patient after each bolus.

### 5.5 Vasopressors

Start noradrenaline if the MAP remains below 65 mmHg during or after fluid resuscitation. Noradrenaline may be started through a large-bore peripheral cannula while central venous access is being arranged.

## 6 Escalation

Call the Critical Care Outreach Team (CCOT) for any patient with a lactate of 4 mmol/L or more, septic shock, a NEWS2 score of 7 or more, or no improvement one hour after the bundle has been completed.

## 7 Documentation

Record time zero and the completion time of each bundle element on the sepsis screening form. The form is audited monthly by the Emergency Medicine quality lead.
