## 1 Purpose

This protocol standardises the prescribing, administration and monitoring of intravenous unfractionated heparin (UFH) infusions for adult patients in DHN intensive care units, so that anticoagulation reaches the target range quickly while the risk of bleeding is controlled and every adjustment is traceable.

## 2 Scope

This protocol applies to adult patients (16 years and older) receiving therapeutic intravenous unfractionated heparin in any DHN intensive care unit. It does not cover paediatric patients, anticoagulation of extracorporeal circuits (ECMO or CRRT circuits) or prophylactic subcutaneous heparin, which are covered by separate protocols.

## 3 Contraindications and precautions

Do not start a heparin infusion without a senior review (ICU consultant or registrar) if any of the following apply:

- active major bleeding, or haemorrhagic stroke within the last 3 months;
- platelet count below 50 x 10^9/L;
- suspected or confirmed heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT);
- neurosurgery or a spinal procedure within the last 7 days.

## 4 Dosing

All doses in this section are weight-based. The prescriber calculates them using the measured or best-estimate body weight and documents the weight used on the infusion chart.

### 4.1 Initial bolus and infusion

- Loading bolus: 80 units/kg IV (maximum 10,000 units). Omit the bolus if the indication is acute ischaemic stroke or the patient is at high risk of bleeding.
- Starting infusion rate: 18 units/kg/h (maximum starting rate 2,000 units/h).
- Standard concentration: 25,000 units in 50 mL sodium chloride 0.9% (500 units/mL), prepared by pharmacy or supplied pre-mixed.

### 4.2 aPTT adjustment nomogram

Adjust the infusion according to each aPTT result using the table below. The therapeutic target is an aPTT of 60–85 seconds.

| aPTT (seconds) | Bolus | Infusion action | Next aPTT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below 40 | 80 units/kg | Increase by 4 units/kg/h | 6 hours |
| 40–59 | 40 units/kg | Increase by 2 units/kg/h | 6 hours |
| 60–85 | None | No change | Next morning |
| 86–100 | None | Decrease by 2 units/kg/h | 6 hours |
| Above 100 | None | Hold the infusion for 60 minutes, then decrease by 2 units/kg/h | 6 hours |

### 4.3 Monitoring

- Repeat the aPTT 6 hours after starting the infusion and 6 hours after every rate change.
- Once two consecutive aPTT results are within the target range, check the aPTT once daily.
- Check the platelet count daily. A fall of more than 50% from baseline requires an assessment for HIT.
- Record every rate change on the infusion chart with the time and the aPTT result that prompted it.

## 5 Independent double check

Heparin is a high-alert medication (see DG-02). Two registered nurses must independently check the prescription, the syringe concentration, the pump programme and the patient's identity at the start of the infusion, at every rate change and at every syringe change. Both nurses sign the infusion chart.

## 6 Bleeding and reversal

- Stop the infusion immediately for any major bleeding and call the ICU consultant.
- Protamine sulfate: 1 mg neutralises approximately 100 units of heparin given in the previous 2 hours (maximum single dose 50 mg). Give by slow IV injection over 10 minutes.
- Recheck the aPTT 15 minutes after protamine is given.

## 7 Escalation

Contact the ICU registrar on call for any aPTT above 150 seconds, any bleeding, or a platelet count below 100 x 10^9/L. Out of hours, the on-call clinical pharmacist advises on infusion preparation and compatibility (see the escalation directory in Lumina).
